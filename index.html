About

I’m a learning designer and researcher, and the question I keep coming back to is a human one: when AI can produce an answer in seconds, how do we help people keep thinking for themselves?

My path into this work started long before generative AI. As an educational technology student in Shanghai, I spent three summers running a maker program for kids at a community charity school, watching them prototype, get stuck, and try again. Later I taught working adults in hybrid courses at Shanghai Open University and helped build a FutureLearn MOOC that reached learners in 156 countries. Every one of those settings taught me the same lesson: good design starts with knowing who your learners are and what they bring with them.

Today I teach future teachers at Syracuse University, and my research grows out of that classroom. I bring AI into my course on purpose, not to hand students shortcuts, but to give them something to question, revise, and sometimes reject. What I watch for each semester is the moment a student stops asking “Can I use AI for this?” and starts asking “Should I use it here?” My research looks at how that shift happens, the anxiety and confidence that come with it, and how learning experiences can be designed to make it more likely.

As an international scholar myself, I pay close attention to learners who are easy to overlook in conversations about AI, including international students, working professionals, and learners with disabilities. For me, accessibility is where design begins, not a box to check at the end.

I will complete my Ph.D. in Instructional Design, Development, and Evaluation in May 2027, advised by Dr. Jing Lei.

Research Areas

Human-Centered AI in Learning Design

Design-based research on how learners develop judgment and agency when AI plays different roles in a learning environment.

Emotion, Efficacy & Technology

How AI-related anxiety, digital self-efficacy, and social-emotional competence shape educators’ engagement with emerging technologies.

Learners, Communities & Context

How educators and learners across cultures and institutions make sense of educational AI, and what that means for accessible, equitable design.
