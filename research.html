Research

Designing for judgment, not substitution

My research asks how learning environments can be designed so that generative AI strengthens the professional judgment and agency of the people who use it. I treat this as a design problem, not only an attitudinal one, and work across design-based research, qualitative inquiry, instrument development, structural equation modeling, and systematic review.

Human-Centered AI and Design-Based Research

My dissertation is a design-based research study embedded in IDE405: Digital Pedagogies and Assistive Technologies, an undergraduate course I have designed and taught since Fall 2025. Across a sequence of projects, generative AI takes on deliberately different roles: idea generator, reflective partner, simulation partner, design assistant, and ethical challenger. Learners are not asked to complete tasks with AI. They are asked to evaluate its output against learning goals and learners’ needs, revise or reject it, explain their reasoning, and name what responsibility must remain with the human designer.

Drawing on reflective journals, interviews, design artifacts, classroom discussion, and fieldnotes across three cohorts, the study traces how learners’ emotional experiences and design decisions develop together, and the course is refined from one cycle to the next. A companion study examines Generation Z pre-service teachers’ own accounts of learning to teach with AI, distinguishing everyday digital fluency from the professional reasoning that AI integration requires.

The Social-Emotional Conditions of Technology Use

Technology integration is usually framed as a matter of knowledge, skill, or access. My second line of work argues that emotions, efficacy beliefs, and social-emotional competence matter just as much, and that they can be measured and designed for.

In a survey study of 406 pre-service teachers, my colleagues and I developed and validated a multidimensional framework of AI-related teaching anxiety and used structural equation modeling to show that social-emotional competence was associated with lower anxiety, both directly and through digital self-efficacy. A systematic scoping review of teacher social-emotional learning (components, frameworks, and interventions) extends this work and received the 2026 AERA SEL SIG Graduate Student Excellent Research Award.

Learners, Communities, and Context

Designs that work in one setting may not transfer to another. A third line of work asks for whom a design works and under what conditions. One qualitative study examines, from a sociocultural perspective, how graduate students in an asynchronous online course construct meaning around educational AI. Another examines how international students use AI chatbots for social-emotional support, language practice, and cultural adaptation, and how that use shapes belonging and well-being. A UDL-grounded audit of AI-generated K–12 classroom images compares prompting strategies and documents patterns of accessibility omissions.

At the policy level, I co-edited two forthcoming Emerald volumes on educational AI policies and practices around the world and wrote a chapter on how U.S. federal AI guidance is translated within an evolving state and district policy ecology.

Next Directions

Which AI roles prompt deliberation?

Testing how AI configured as a Socratic questioner, reflective mirror, stakeholder simulator, or ethical challenger shapes reasoning and overreliance.

Judgment beyond the course

Following learners from coursework into the schools, workplaces, and communities where they practice.

Accessible AI-supported design

Developing design heuristics that help designers evaluate AI-generated learning materials for accessibility and representation.
