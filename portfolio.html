Face-to-face course design · Design-based research

Generative AI–Supported Design Projects for Pre-Service Teachers

  • IDE405, Syracuse University
  • Fall 2025 – present
  • 3 design iterations
Challenge
Pre-service teachers are fluent with consumer technology, but fluency does not make them able to judge whether an AI-generated suggestion is accurate, inclusive, or right for a particular learner.
Learners
Undergraduate pre-service teachers in a course on digital pedagogies and assistive technologies.
My role
Course designer, instructor, and researcher.
Design
A sequence of projects tied to field placements, in which AI plays different roles: idea generator, reflective partner, simulation partner, design assistant, and ethical challenger. Students must evaluate AI output, revise or reject it, and justify their decisions. Peer critique of drafts and structured reflection prompts are built in, and UDL and assistive technologies run through every project.
Iteration
The project sequence, scaffolds, and reflection prompts were revised across three semesters based on student work, course evaluations, and reflective data. The course is also the site of my dissertation research.

Asynchronous online course design

Redesigning a Graduate Online Course Around Educational AI

  • IDE772, Syracuse University
  • Summers 2024–2026
  • Blackboard
Challenge
A fast six-week asynchronous course had to address a rapidly changing topic without overloading learners.
Learners
About 20 graduate students each summer, many of them working professionals or international students.
My role
Teaching assistant and course developer.
Design
Modules redesigned around AI applications across educational and professional settings, ethical issues, and international perspectives on educational AI policy. Discussions are framed as problems to work through, such as evaluating an AI-generated resource against a specific learner need, and a consistent weekly rhythm tells learners what to read, produce, and ask.
Lesson learned
Adding content does not make a course stronger. Pacing, workload, and feedback build presence in an asynchronous course.

Accessibility · Evaluating AI-generated materials

A UDL-Grounded Audit of AI-Generated Classroom Images

  • Student PI, Syracuse University
  • 2026 – present
  • AECT 2026
Question
When designers use AI to generate images of classrooms, which learners and supports become visible, and which are left out?
Approach
A binary coding checklist operationalizing inclusive classroom design (physical access, information access, assistive technology, and participation supports), applied to images produced under baseline, bias-aware, checklist-constrained, and plan-then-generate prompts.
Why it matters
Preliminary patterns show accessibility omissions and differences in visible supports across prompting strategies, pointing toward practical heuristics designers can use when they evaluate AI-generated learning materials.
